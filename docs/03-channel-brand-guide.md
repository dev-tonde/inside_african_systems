# Inside African Systems — Channel Brand Guide

## Brand foundation

### Purpose

Help Africans and the world understand the systems shaping everyday life across the continent.

### Promise

Every episode explains one important company, infrastructure network, market, or public system with evidence, original visual storytelling, and clear language.

### Position

Inside African Systems is not breaking news, investment advice, corporate publicity, or generic “Africa is rising” content. It is an independent visual-documentary channel interested in mechanisms, incentives, tradeoffs, and consequences.

### Audience

Primary:

- Africans aged roughly 18–44 who are curious about business, technology and infrastructure
- Professionals, students, founders and globally minded viewers

Secondary:

- International viewers seeking grounded African business stories
- Diaspora audiences
- Educators and journalists looking for accessible visual explanations

### Brand values

- Evidence before confidence
- Clarity without simplification
- African agency
- Independence
- Fairness
- Craft
- Curiosity

## Verbal identity

### Voice

Intelligent, calm, observant, precise and accessible.

### Sound like

- “The interesting part is not the app. It is the system behind the app.”
- “That number matters, but it does not tell the whole story.”
- “To understand the outcome, follow the incentives.”

### Do not sound like

- “You won’t believe this insane secret.”
- “This company destroyed everyone.”
- “Africa is the future” without evidence or qualification.
- Corporate press-release language.
- Cynical narration that treats African problems as spectacle.

### Naming formula

Favor:

- How [system] actually works
- How [company] built [advantage]
- Why [outcome] happened
- The hidden system behind [familiar experience]
- [Company A] vs [Company B]: [specific contest]

Avoid guaranteed outcomes, accusations, or absolute superlatives the evidence cannot support.

## Visual identity

### Logo concept

The starter mark combines:

- A circle for a complete system
- A connected pulse/route for movement and cause-and-effect
- An interruption in the line for investigation and tension

It must work in one colour before decorative versions are developed.

### Colour palette

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Ink | `#081C1A` | Primary background; seriousness and depth |
| Archive Paper | `#F4EEDF` | Light background; documents and evidence |
| Mineral Sand | `#D8B26E` | Historical context and secondary emphasis |
| Signal Ember | `#E85D3F` | Hooks, warnings, movement and key numbers |
| System Teal | `#0F766E` | Data, infrastructure and trusted emphasis |
| Coastal Sky | `#85B9B2` | Lines, networks and supporting graphics |
| Neutral Stone | `#9C9A8E` | Metadata, sources and secondary copy |

Do not use every colour in every frame. Deep Ink, Archive Paper and one accent should dominate.

### Typography

Use open fonts with strong African-language character coverage.

Recommended:

- Headline: **Archivo Black** or **Manrope ExtraBold**
- Body and labels: **Inter**
- Data and source IDs: **IBM Plex Mono**

The code starter currently uses system fonts so it can render before font assets are introduced.

### Type scale at 1920×1080

| Use | Typical size |
|---|---:|
| Documentary title | 96–132 px |
| Scene question | 64–88 px |
| Key statistic | 100–150 px |
| Section heading | 34–48 px |
| Labels | 22–30 px |
| Source line | 18–22 px |
| Captions | 38–48 px |

Keep important text inside a 110 px horizontal and 76 px vertical safe area.

### Graphic language

Use:

- Network lines
- Route maps
- Layered timelines
- Flow diagrams
- Documentary source cards
- Bold number treatments
- Archive-paper panels
- Clean geometric cropping

Avoid:

- Generic glowing AI globes
- Unrelated skyline montages
- Fake newspaper pages
- Decorative charts without units or sources
- Excessive flags
- Stereotypical “African” patterning used without cultural specificity

## Motion language

Motion should reveal relationships.

- Nodes appear when introduced in narration.
- Lines connect causes and consequences.
- Maps animate by route or expansion order.
- Charts grow only after labels and units are visible.
- Timelines move consistently left to right.
- Cuts accelerate during the hook and settle during explanation.

Typical timing:

- Hook cuts: 1–3 seconds
- Explanatory scenes: 4–8 seconds
- Charts and diagrams: 6–12 seconds
- Major question card: 3–5 seconds

Avoid random floating objects, constant zooming, aggressive glitch effects, and transitions that do not explain a change.

## Thumbnail system

Use one dominant idea:

- Recognizable company/system object
- One tension
- Two to five words
- Strong light/dark contrast
- One accent colour

Examples:

- `THE 60-MINUTE MACHINE`
- `WHY CAPITEC WON`
- `TAKEALOT vs AMAZON`
- `WHO RUNS THE ROUTES?`

Test at phone size. Never publish a thumbnail promise that the episode does not prove.

## Audio identity

- One consistent narrator during Season 1
- Controlled, documentary pacing
- No imitation of a real public figure
- Music supports tension without overpowering speech
- Prefer subtle percussion, texture and restrained tonal beds
- Keep an auditable licence record for every music track and sound effect

## Episode identity

Opening:

1. Supported hook
2. Country/season marker
3. Central question

Closing:

1. Direct answer
2. Remaining limitation
3. Wider African connection
4. Channel mark

## Geographic expansion

Keep the master identity consistent. Change only country labels, map focus, local pronunciation rules, and culturally relevant supporting colours when justified. Do not redesign the channel for every country.
